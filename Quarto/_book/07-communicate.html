7  Communicate

Translating modeling results into actionable insights through interactive dashboards

7.1 Delivery Format

The findings from this analysis will be delivered through an interactive Shiny dashboard, which integrates:

  • Dynamic aging curve exploration for individual athletes and population-level trends

  • Trelliscope panel plots for side-by-side comparison across multiple athletes and events

7.2 Overview

The final insights will be provided to key stakeholders, including coaches, performance analysts, sports scientists, and sports development directors. The Shiny dashboard will serve as a centralized platform for exploring aging curves, identifying cluster patterns, and comparing event-specific performance trends.

Because aging curves evolve over time and are tied to an athlete’s age, the dashboard will be updated annually to maintain accuracy and relevance. This will allow stakeholders to track changes in trajectories, compare against historical benchmarks, and adjust training or selection strategies accordingly.

By providing accessible, data-driven insights, the dashboard will support evidence-based decision-making in training program design, athlete selection, and long-term talent development planning.

7.3 Clusters interpretation

Three primary aging curve patterns are identified through clustering:

  • Early risers: Rapid gains early in a career, reaching a plateau sooner

  • Steady performers: Consistent year-over-year improvements, leading to a smooth peak

  • Late bloomers: Gradual early progress followed by substantial improvement later in the career

When clustering is performed by event type, most disciplines show patterns similar to the overall population clusters. However, certain events, such as Middle Distance and Road Marathon(??? double check), display noticeably different cluster structures.

7.5 Key insights for Stakeholders

Performance evolves with age: Although the population mean curve shows the general trend, each athlete’s performance path is unique. Principal components highlight these differences, revealing whether an athlete peaks early, improves steadily, or peaks later in their career.

Cluster shapes are actionable: Knowing whether an athlete is an early improver, steady performer, or late bloomer can guide training focus and competition scheduling.

World Athlete Championships (WAC) context is informative: Comparing an athlete’s performance to world-class benchmarks (WAC mean) helps put their achievements into perspective across different ages and events.

7.6 Limitations and Considerations

Data sparsity: Predictions depend on smoothing methods. Athletes with very limited career data will have wider uncertainty bands around their estimated curves.

Join granularity: Scoring uses a “closest less-than-or-equal-to” rule for matching performances to reference tables. For athletes whose results are close to table knots, small shifts can occur in assigned point values.

Causality: Aging curves describe associations between performance and age. They do not imply causal effects of age, training, or other factors on performance changes.

7.7 Next steps

Event-type stratification: Fit separate FACE models for each event type (for example, sprints, middle distance, throws) and compare their mean aging curves to determine whether different disciplines follow distinct performance trajectories.

Uncertainty visualization: Incorporate visualizations of individual confidence intervals within the Shiny dashboard’s comparison view so that coaches can weigh the reliability of predicted trajectories.

Trajectory monitoring: Implement year-over-year tracking of within-athlete shifts in curve shape (for example, an early peak evolving into a stable phase) and generate alerts when an athlete moves to a different cluster.

Robust clustering: Evaluate robust clustering methods such as trimmed k-means to improve stability in the presence of outliers, and refine the number of clusters using within-cluster sum of squares (SSE) methods as described in the reference paper.

Selection bias adjustment: Address potential right-tail selection bias, as higher-performing athletes tend to remain in competition longer, which may skew the observed distribution of aging curves.
